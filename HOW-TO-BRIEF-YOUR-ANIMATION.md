# 🎬 How to Brief Your Animation (the easy way)

You don't need to know any technical words. Type **blend**, then give whatever level
you have — from a one-line idea to a full script. Copy-paste any template below.

---

## Level 0 — Just an idea (1 line is enough)

> blend — Animate a tired old robot who finds a small flower and tries to protect it.

Claude will design the story, shots and style, show you a short **Story Card** summary,
and only start after you say OK.

---

## Level 1 — The Mini Brief ⭐ (recommended — copy this)

```
blend
IDEA: (what happens, 1–3 lines)
WHO: (characters + one line about each)
FEELING: (what should the viewer feel? even one word)
STYLE: (pick: realistic / stylized feature / cartoon / anime / stop-motion feel /
        game cutscene / product film — or say "like this film: ___" or "you choose")
LENGTH + WHERE: (e.g. 30 seconds, YouTube)
I ALREADY HAVE: (models? Blender file? images? music? — or "nothing")
```

**Filled example:**
```
blend
IDEA: A night watchman robot in a dead city finds the last living flower.
      He is too big and clumsy, so he struggles to water it without crushing it.
WHO: Rusty old security robot, gentle heart. One small flower.
FEELING: wonder turning into tenderness
STYLE: like a Pixar short (stylized feature)
LENGTH + WHERE: about 60 seconds, YouTube
I ALREADY HAVE: nothing
```

---

## Level 2 — Add scenes (if you know them)

Add to the Mini Brief:

```
SCENES:
1. ...
2. ...
MUST HAVE: (moments/objects that must appear)
MUST AVOID: (no violence / no dialogue / etc.)
DIALOGUE: (exact lines, if any)
```

---

## Level 3 — Write your own story (simple structure that always works)

Every good animation, of any length, runs on this spine:

1. **Setup** — normal life.
2. **Something breaks the balance** (the "inciting incident").
3. **Struggle** — hero tries → fails in an unexpected way → tries harder (repeat, bigger).
4. **Crisis** — the hardest choice.
5. **Climax** — the final answer to the struggle.
6. **Ending** — new normal + the feeling you leave behind.

**Copy-paste story template:**

```
TITLE:
LOGLINE: (hero) wants (goal) but (problem)...
HERO: wants ___ · really needs ___ · weakness/habit ___
THE DAY EVERYTHING CHANGES (inciting incident):
BEATS (what happens, one line each):
  1. tries ___ → but unexpectedly ___
  2. tries ___ → but ___
  3. tries ___ → but ___
HARDEST CHOICE (crisis):
CLIMAX (final big moment):
ENDING FEELING: "life becomes ___ when you ___"
```

The magic ingredient in every beat is the **"but unexpectedly…"** — when what happens
is different from what the hero expected, the story turns and the audience leans in.
No "but" = boring beat.

## Level 4 — Shot list (if you think in camera)

```
# | seconds | what we see | camera | sound / dialogue
1 | 5 | empty city, robot patrolling | wide, slow push-in | wind, distant hum
2 | 4 | his foot stops just before the flower | close, low angle | metallic squeak
```

## Level 5 — Full script

Any rough format works. Scene heading + present-tense action + dialogue:

```
EXT. RUINED STREET – DAWN
RUSTY, an old robot, patrols with a squeaky wheel. He stops.
A single FLOWER glows between the cracks.
RUSTY (soft beep, amazed)
...alive?
```

---

## What Claude does with your brief (so there are no surprises)

1. Shows you a **Story Card** (one-screen summary) → you approve or correct the feeling.
2. Designs a **fresh pipeline** for this project + explains it in simple words.
3. Works in stages (poses story → smooth motion → detail → light/texture → render),
   checking each stage itself before showing you.
4. Remembers every correction you give in a lessons file — **you never repeat a note.**

## How to give feedback (the magic format)

> **Shot 3 — he looks angry; I wanted sad.**

Just say *where + what you see + what you wanted to feel*. No technical words needed.

## Things you never need to worry about

Rig types, polygons, texture sizes, frame rates, render settings, perfect English,
fancy formatting. That's the skill's job. Your job is only: **what happens + how it
should feel**. One helpful extra: mention your PC (graphics card/RAM) once — Claude
will keep renders within your machine's power without spoiling the realism.

## 🔧 Keep the toolbox fresh — the magic word "toolcheck"

New tools for smoother animation and better textures will keep coming — the skill
is built for that. Anytime (especially after updating Blender, or when you see an
interesting add-on/AI tool in a video), just type:

> **blend toolcheck**  — or —  **blend, check this tool: (name/link)**

Claude will test the new tool against strict quality rules from the books (smooth
arcs? no foot-slide? legal texture values?), compare it fairly with the current
method, and give you a simple verdict: **Adopt / Trial / Park / Reject — and why**.
The decision is written into a `TOOLS.md` file in your project, so the skill
remembers it forever and keeps getting current without chasing hype.
